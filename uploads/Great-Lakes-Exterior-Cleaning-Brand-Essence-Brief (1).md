# Brand Essence Brief — Great Lakes Exterior Cleaning

## 1. Essence
The neighbor who happens to be the most careful person in the trade — professional enough to trust, human enough to wave at from the driveway.

## 2. The Visitor and the Job
A Buffalo-area homeowner looking at their siding, windows, or a pile of junk they've been putting off, feeling mildly embarrassed and a little overwhelmed about who to call. In five seconds they need to believe: *this crew shows up, communicates, and treats my house like it's theirs.* The site exists to get them to request a quote — fast, low-friction, and answered by a real person soon after.

## 3. Values

- **Responsiveness** — fast quotes, real answers, no phone-tag. Visual consequence: contact/quote action is never more than one scroll away; response-time promise stated plainly, not buried.
- **Meticulous craft** — the detail work sloppier competitors skip (edging, trim, downspouts, not just the big flat surfaces). Visual consequence: before/after imagery has to show close-up detail, not just wide "clean house" shots.
- **Trustworthy locality** — this is Buffalo, run by someone from Buffalo, not a franchise call center. Visual consequence: real photos of real WNY houses/streets, not stock suburbia; a personal, named-owner presence somewhere on the site.
- **Warmth over slickness** — human-first, not corporate-first. Visual consequence: some imperfection is allowed — real photos of the crew, not polished lifestyle stock; copy voice stays plain-spoken, not "solutions"-speak.
- **Quiet high standards** — professional and premium-adjacent without being showy about it. Visual consequence: restrained palette, clean structure, no discount-bin badges or urgency banners.

## 5. Non-Negotiables
- Service area stated plainly (Buffalo + Western New York), ideally with recognizable local reference points, not just "we serve your area"
- Before/after photos — real jobs, real WNY houses, close-up enough to show edge/detail work
- A quote request path that's fast and clearly promises a real response time (e.g., "quote within 24 hours")
- Phone number visible in the header, not buried in a footer
- Insurance/licensing mention if applicable — this trade has a credibility problem with fly-by-night operators, and stating this plainly closes that gap
- Real reviews, ideally with names/neighborhoods, not just star ratings
- Some visible presence of the owner/crew — a face or team photo, not just logos and icons

## 6. Constraints

- **Color:** Must carry the existing navy + green identity. Navy does the "professional, trustworthy" work; green should feel more like a considered accent than a full second brand color. Off-limits: bright primary red/orange (reads like a discount or hazard-alert service), gradients, neon accents. Nothing that reads corporate-cold (sterile grey-on-white with navy reduced to a logo mark only).
- **Type:** Needs to read as clean and professional at a glance, and legible to an older homeowner audience — no ultra-thin display faces, no tight tracking on body copy. Current, not stiff-corporate or trade-show generic.
- **Imagery:** Real photos of real houses, real before/afters, real crew — the single biggest lever for the "human, in the moment" feeling. Cheapens instantly: generic stock photos of smiling homeowners, clipart-style service icons, staged "family in front of house" stock.
- **Motion:** Light and purposeful is fine (smooth scroll reveals, subtle hover states) — this brand wants "breathtaking." But motion should never feel decorative for its own sake or slow the site down; nothing bouncy, nothing that delays getting to the quote button.
- **Density:** Moderate-lean-sparse. Let photography breathe, but proof elements (reviews, service area, phone number) must stay easy to find.

## 7. Wrong for This Brand
- Discount/urgency badges, "% OFF" banners, countdown-style promos — undercuts "highest standards"
- Cartoonish service icons (spray bottle clipart, cartoon truck) — reads cheap, not premium-adjacent
- Cold, minimal B2B-SaaS aesthetic — this isn't a tech product, it needs warmth
- Overly rustic/folksy styling (barnwood textures, hand-lettered scripts) — wrong register for "professional, highest standards"
- Aggressive red/black "storm damage emergency" styling — that's a different kind of home-services brand, not this one
- Wall-to-wall stock photography — instantly breaks the "human, in the moment" promise

## 8. Visual Directions

### 1. Navy Daylight
Clean, bright, confident — navy header/accents against generous white space, full-bleed real photography doing the emotional work. Feels like a well-run local company that also happens to have great photos.
- Light, cool-neutral base with navy as the anchor color, green as a small accent
- Modern humanist sans for headers, clean grotesk for body
- Soft-to-moderate corners, moderate shadow
- Generous spacing, photography-led

### 2. Field Green
Flips the accent hierarchy — green takes the lead as the "fresh, cared-for exterior" color, navy recedes to text and structure. Slightly more outdoorsy/seasonal, still professional.
- Light, warm-neutral base, green-forward with navy as grounding accent
- Same type family logic as Navy Daylight but slightly warmer body tone
- Soft corners, airy layout

*Distinctness check: differs from Navy Daylight on color balance/warmth and accent hierarchy — passes, since it changes which color leads and shifts warm/cool, not just hue.*

### 3. Ledger & Trust
Denser, more document-like — the "prove it" direction. Structured grid, clear sections, review counts and before/afters presented almost like a portfolio/spec sheet.
- Light background, navy as structural color (rules, dividers, headers), green as accent only
- Condensed or slab-leaning sans for headers vs. a plain grotesk body
- Sharper corners, tighter spacing, more information-dense

*Distinctness check vs. Navy Daylight/Field Green: differs on density (tight vs. generous) and corner language (sharp vs. soft). Passes.*

### 4. Night Crew
Dark direction — navy deepened toward near-black as the dominant surface, green and white used sparingly as bright accents. Photography (before/afters, dusk exterior shots) pops hard against the dark ground. Premium and a little cinematic, without losing plainspoken trade-business copy.
- Dark, cool base; green as a bright, sparing accent
- Same humanist sans family, high-contrast against dark
- Soft corners, moderate density, photography given room to breathe

*Distinctness check vs. #1: differs on light/dark and mood — passes on its own.*

### 5. Porch Light *(the direction the owner probably wouldn't reach for)*
Warmer and more editorial-magazine than trade-website — cream/warm-white base instead of cool white, navy paired with warm neutrals, imagery treated like a home-and-garden feature rather than a service ad. This is where "breathtaking and warm" gets pushed furthest.
- Light, warm-neutral base (not cool white) with navy as accent, green used sparingly
- A slightly more editorial serif or serif-adjacent header face paired with a plain sans body — the one direction that isn't all-sans
- Soft corners, generous spacing, sparse and photography-forward

*Distinctness check vs. #1 and #2: differs on typeface category (serif-leaning headers) and warm-neutral vs. cool-neutral base. Passes.*

### 6. Site Standard
The most utilitarian of the set — closer to how a well-run trade company actually presents itself: navy header bar, clear service cards, phone number and quote button always visible, photography present but functioning more as proof than as art.
- Light, cool-neutral base, navy-dominant with green as a functional accent (buttons, checkmarks)
- Plain grotesk throughout, no display face
- Sharper corners, tight/businesslike density, sticky header with phone/CTA always visible

*Distinctness check vs. Ledger & Trust: both denser, but this is sans-only/functional vs. Ledger's condensed-header "document" feel and heavier rule/divider structure. Passes.*

**Spread check:** #1, #2, #5 light · #4 dark · #3, #6 dense/utilitarian · #5 is the outlier direction.
